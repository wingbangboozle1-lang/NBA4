# NBA line-origination and signal framework

**Status: research specification only. No strategy has been trained, backtested, validated, or approved.** The arithmetic and control logic behind this specification is implemented and unit-tested in [`engine/lineorigination.py`](../engine/lineorigination.py), [`engine/parlay.py`](../engine/parlay.py), [`engine/correlation.py`](../engine/correlation.py) and [`engine/validation.py`](../engine/validation.py); see [`docs/line-origination.md`](line-origination.md) and [`docs/correlation-and-leaderboard.md`](correlation-and-leaderboard.md). The engine contains no NBA or market data and has never produced a forecast from real inputs. The repository has no authorized player/game history, schedule feed, injuries-by-timestamp, market prices, or settlement results. I will not fabricate current lines, picks, probabilities, correlations, injury-minute effects, or a “top strategy.”

## Hard data-rights gate

The NBA Stats FAQ says its statistics are not available for download for academic or personal use. NBA.com Terms of Use further restrict use of NBA Statistics in gambling, including legal gambling, and require express prior consent for a comprehensive, regularly updated NBA statistics database. The NBA glossary below is cited only as a public definition source; it is **not** a license or authorization to collect, reuse, or feed those statistics into betting models. Before any NBA-derived input, obtain written permission and a data agreement that explicitly covers betting use, model features/derivatives, storage, historical access, public display, and the relevant jurisdictions—or use a separately licensed source whose contract expressly permits those uses. Until then, the output is “no picks.”

Sources: [NBA Stats FAQ](https://www.nba.com/stats/help/faq) · [NBA.com Terms of Use, §§1 and 9](https://www.nba.com/termsofuse)

## Signal construction and line comparison

A model-originated line must be separate from an available market quote. For a continuous stat, store a predictive distribution and derive the fair threshold/quantile requested by the market; for a binary outcome, store a calibrated probability. A potential “edge” is the model probability/price comparison only after the market, timing, settlement and vig/fee conventions are defined and the input rights permit use. None of those current price fields is present here, so all such values stay blank.

Every forecast record should be immutable and contain: event/market/participant IDs; model and code version; input snapshot IDs and source timestamps; the time the forecast was emitted; forecast mean/distribution or probability; model-originated line; benchmark quote and its timestamp if licensed; settlement/void/push rule; eventual result; and audit status. Retain corrections as new versions rather than overwriting the original forecast. Do not use a later injury, final lineup, closing price, or result as an earlier feature.

## Correlation plan for combos

“Correlation” is not a fixed player property. Estimate it for a defined pair of legs, threshold/market type, conditioning set, model version, season/window and point-in-time data policy. Use residuals (observed outcome minus the model’s pregame expectation) for continuous production relationships, and direct joint-event frequencies/probabilities for threshold legs. Report sample count and uncertainty beside every estimate. A Pearson coefficient summarizes linear association and ranges from −1 to +1; it does not prove causality or give a parlay’s full joint probability. Bootstrap uncertainty by game/date clusters so multiple legs from one game are not treated as unrelated observations. Shrink unstable pair estimates toward zero or a population prior selected before testing; specify that rule and its fit data before the holdout.

The table is intentionally empty where the required data are missing. It is a measurement specification, not a claim that the NBA has a particular correlation. The estimators, the game-cluster bootstrap interval and the preregistered shrinkage rule are implemented in [`engine/correlation.py`](../engine/correlation.py) and [`engine/parlay.py`](../engine/parlay.py); the full method, the estimator table and the leaderboard design are in [`docs/correlation-and-leaderboard.md`](correlation-and-leaderboard.md).

## Reducing luck / selecting skill

No method can remove randomness from a basketball game or guarantee future profit. The defensible goal is to prevent hindsight, leakage, repeated tuning and tiny-sample noise from being mistaken for skill:

1. Define each strategy, market, feature set, threshold, reward metric and holdout protocol before evaluating it.
2. Compare against simple, reproducible baselines and, only with properly licensed timestamped prices, a declared market benchmark. Never use a closing quote as a feature for an earlier forecast.
3. Use chronological walk-forward tests; keep a final future holdout untouched until design choices are frozen. The official scikit-learn guidance explains why time-series evaluation should test future observations rather than train on future and evaluate the past.
4. Report probability calibration and proper scores (for example, Brier/log loss), line error, net returns after the actual payout/cost rules, and uncertainty. A Brier score is a probability score, not a standalone proof of calibration or profitability.
5. Quantify uncertainty by game/date resampling, run sensitivity/ablation checks, and account for the number of strategy variants examined. Do not promote a strategy just because its best backtest or short-term ROI is high.
6. Lock each forecast and stake-sizing rule before the result. A strategy is ineligible if records are edited, data timestamps are missing, the test has leaked, or rights/compliance gates fail.

Sources: [scikit-learn time-series cross-validation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/cross_validation.html) · [scikit-learn model metrics / Brier score](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/model_evaluation.html) · [NIST bootstrap guidance](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/bootplot.htm)

## Leaderboard and reward design

Leaderboard eligibility should be a gate, not a prize for the largest unadjusted backtest. Display each candidate’s frozen version, market, sample size/window, validation status, calibration, net outcome after the specified costs, uncertainty, drawdown/risk, baseline comparison and correlation/combination metrics. Rank only candidates that pass every pre-registered gate. Do not set a composite score, minimum sample, stake, reward amount, ties, or payment cadence until the product owner sets the objective/risk policy and legal review confirms the reward model and jurisdictions. Until then, show **no ranked winners and no rewards**.

The gate-aware ranking logic (only gate-passing, version-locked candidates are ranked; a gate that is not reported counts as failed) is implemented in [`engine/leaderboard.py`](../engine/leaderboard.py). A leaderboard must not imply that past results guarantee future outcomes. The public site should label every proposed strategy “unvalidated,” suppress numeric picks/edge/ROI for a failed or incomplete gate, and keep a review/audit history for changes. If rewards are monetary or inducive, obtain jurisdiction-specific advice before offering them.
