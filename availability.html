Rotation availability by team

The brief asks for rotation availability as its own column, and for an explicit top-8 minutes-share redistribution when a player is out. This page publishes exactly that much and stops: the designations are restated from the official report, the top-8 share is printed as an assumption with its value visible, and the player-level minutes estimate is refused with the reason. No number here is a forecast of a player’s minutes.

i
What the league publishes, and what it does not.

An injury report prints a player, a team, a status (Out, Questionable, Available, or nothing) and a reason. It prints no minutes, no usage and no lineup. A minutes projection therefore needs a second source that this repository does not hold under a cleared right, so engine/rotations.py takes the replacement weights from the caller and refuses when they are absent. The share below is a stated assumption, not a fitted parameter.

Reading the columns. Out, Questionable and Available are the league’s own words, counted. Top-8 minutes share assumed is 0.88 for every team: that is, the eight heaviest-used players are assumed to take 88% of the team’s minutes. It is an assumption recorded in scripts/build_snapshots.py (ROTATION_TOP8_SHARE) so a reader can change it and recompute, and engine/minutesshare.py will measure the real share from a box score once an authorised one is available. Replacement share if the named players sat subtracts 0.04 per unavailable player from that assumed share — a visible placeholder for the redistribution, not a fitted model. Player-level minutes estimate is gated.

Reading the columns. Days rest is the gap between this game and the team’s previous printed game date — a date arithmetic on the document, not an elapsed-hours claim; the schedule prints a date and a tip time but no travel itinerary. Back-to-back is days_rest == 1, which is the repo’s declared rule and is printed so it can be disputed. Games in last 4 days counts the four-day window ending on the game date, and the flag is raised at three. Rest differential is this team’s days rest minus the opponent’s. Travel distance would need the arena of the previous game, which is only printed on a game page — for the eleven games on 11/16 the previous venue is available from the 11/15 results, but 11/17–11/18 legs would require game pages that do not exist yet, so the column is uniformly refused rather than filled for some rows and not others.

i
One measured consequence worth reading.

Of the 50 team-entries in the 11/16–11/18 window, 11 are a second game in two nights (Boston, Washington, Philadelphia, Houston and Phoenix on 11/16; Portland and Minnesota on 11/17; Toronto, Detroit, Utah and the Lakers on 11/18) and 15 are a third game in four days. Every one of those is a printed-date fact from the schedule PDF, re-derivable from the file data/snapshots/schedule-window-2026-11-13-to-18.json; none of it is a forecast.

How the redistribution is specified

Minutes are a fixed budget: 240 team-minutes in regulation, plus five for each overtime period. When a player’s minutes fall, an equal amount must appear elsewhere, so the model is a redistribution of a constant, never a rescaling.

  1. Removed minutes = the sum of the shortfall of each unavailable player.
  2. New share(p) = old share(p) + removed × weight(p) / ∑ weights.
  3. The weights are coach-behaviour parameters supplied by the caller and never defaulted here.

Validity checks in engine/rotations.py: shares non-negative, no share above the game length, and a top-8 group that cannot exceed the team budget.

What is refused, and why

  • Bet surface nothing on this page is a price, a stake, an edge or a pick.
  • Player-level minutes: refused. No authorised minute-allocation history exists, so an estimate would be invented.
  • Availability probability: refused. A status is a designation, not a probability; the repo holds no point-in-time history to calibrate one.
  • Production impact: refused. That needs a validated role → minute → production chain, which does not exist yet.
